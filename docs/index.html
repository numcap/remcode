Built for the way you already practice.

          
Solve problems on LeetCode or NeetCode as usual. ReMCode handles the saving and the scheduling.

        


        

          

            
          
          
          
          
          

          
        


        


        

          

            
Saved the moment it's accepted

            
When a submission passes, a card opens over the page with the problem, the exact code you submitted and its runtime.

            
Write one line you'll want to read later, then press 1–4 to rate it. Nothing to copy or paste.

          

          
        


        

          

            
Recall it before you look

            
Reviews show the problem statement first. Work out the pattern, the invariant and a sketch of the code in your head.

            
A review takes a minute or two instead of the twenty it took to solve.

          

          
        


        

          

            
Check your own notes and code

            
Reveal the insight and solution you saved, then rate how well you remembered.

            
Each rating shows when you'll see the problem next. FSRS picks the interval from your history with that card.

          

          
        


        

          

            
Every problem, in one place

            
Search by title, number, topic or your own notes. Filter by site and difficulty, or sort by what's due next.

            
Back up to a file, or sync to a private GitHub repository you own.

          

          
        

        

      

    


    

      

        

          

            
Your deck stays yours.

            
Check what's due from the toolbar, or review on your phone with Firefox for Android.

          

          

            

              
Local only

              
No account and no server. Your deck lives in the browser's extension storage.

            

            

              
Spaced repetition that adapts

              
The FSRS algorithm schedules each problem close to the point where you'd start to forget it.
